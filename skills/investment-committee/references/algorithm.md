# Committee algorithm — detail

A quantitative score from 0 to 100, always computed the same way, to discipline the team's judgement (not to replace it). Four pillars built on well-studied long-term factors: **quality** (ROIC, gross profit/assets — Novy-Marx), **value** ((FCF − SBC)/market cap, EBIT/EV — Greenblatt), **health** (Piotroski F-score, net debt/EBITDA) and **dividend** (payout on FCF; left out if the company pays no dividend).

- The inputs come from each analyst's "Data for the algorithm" block, already confirmed by the `data-verifier`: `fundamental-analyst` (ROIC, F-score, gross profit/assets, net debt/EBITDA), `valuation-analyst` (EBIT/EV, (FCF − SBC)/market cap) and `dividend-analyst` (payout on FCF).
- **One record per company per calendar year** (the first): recording the same company several times would count it twice in the IC. `calc.py` refuses a second record in the same year.
- Whenever the committee scores a company, run `calc.py score … --price … --currency … --index-name … --index … --record`. The index is the **local total-return index in the stock's currency** (Switzerland: SPI; USA: S&P 500 Total Return; euro area: EURO STOXX 50 Net Return; UK: FTSE All-Share TR; other countries: the main total-return index of the local exchange), so exchange rates play no part in the measurement. Show the score in the report next to the team's score. **If the two differ by more than 2 points (algorithm/10 vs. team), explain why** — that is where the biases, or what the numbers miss, are found.
- **12-month outcome:** when a record turns 12 months old, `calc.py close` stores the price and dividends per share (on the share basis of the record date: if a 2:1 split happened after the record, × 2; see `references/data.md`) and the index **on that exact date** (not today's). A fixed horizon keeps the years comparable.
- **Learning** (`calc.py learn`): for each year, it measures the **IC** (rank correlation between the score and the excess return over the local index, within that year) of each pillar and each metric. It then pools the years: mean IC, t-test and % of years with the same sign. It only proposes changing a pillar's weight when the IC is significant (p < 0.10) over **3+ years** with **5+ records each**; new weight = weight × (1 + mean IC). If nothing is significant, the answer is "keep the weights" — and that is a valid answer. Weights only change with the user's approval.
- The IC per metric (ROIC, F-score, payout…) shows whether a metric is not working as expected. In that case the committee proposes that the user review that metric's thresholds, rather than touching the weights.
