# Periodic workflows (review, tax report, annual letter, dashboard)

They follow the same rules as `SKILL.md` (single source of truth, `calc.py consistency` at the start, verifier before decisions).

## "do the review"
1. In `journal.md`, take the decisions older than 3 months (excluding the REVIEW, BUY EXECUTED and SELL EXECUTED rows). If there are none, say so and skip to step 4.
   **End date:** today by default. For a review with a past end date (e.g. 31/12 for the annual letter), use that date's prices, exchange rates, index and cash, only the transactions up to that date, and write that date in the REVIEW row.
2. For each decision, compare with what happened: current price (converted to the share basis of the decision date if there was a split: see `references/data.md`) vs. the price on the day, dividends, relevant news, and **vs. the local index over the same period** (stock total return − local index total return, in the stock's currency, as in the committee algorithm). If the decision's horizon has not arrived yet, judge only the reasoning and mark it "in progress". Did the verifiable prediction come true? Use the `news-analyst` (group several tickers) and, if needed, the `dividend-analyst`.
3. Classify each one: **deserved success**, **lucky success**, **unlucky mistake** or **reasoning mistake**. The quality of the reasoning counts more than the short-term outcome. For each, explain what caused it.
4. **Performance vs. benchmark:** run `calc.py performance --start V0 --end V1 --days N --flows day:amount,… --index I0,I1`. V0 = the `value_base` of the OPENING_BALANCE without a ticker in the first review (it already includes positions and cash: add nothing; without that row and without inherited positions, V0 = 0 and the period starts at the first DEPOSIT; if there is an OPENING_BALANCE with a ticker and none without, stop and ask the user for the starting value: never use V0 = 0 in that case), or the V1 of the last review; V1 = current value of the positions **plus the cash** at the broker, in the base currency (`calc.py risk --cash …`; ask the user for the cash balance or take it from the statement: without the cash, uninvested deposits, dividends and sale proceeds are left out and the return comes out lower). **Check the cash first:** starting cash = the one in the note of the OPENING_BALANCE without a ticker, or that of the last REVIEW row, or 0 if V0 = 0; expected cash = starting cash + deposits − withdrawals − purchases − commissions + sales + net dividends since V0 (from `transactions.csv`); if it is very different from the cash the user reports, show both values and ask whether a DEPOSIT or WITHDRAWAL is missing, before calculating. N = days in the period; each DEPOSIT is a positive flow and each WITHDRAWAL a negative one, with `day` = days since the start (dividends and purchases are not flows). Compare with the policy benchmark over the same period. Also show the total costs (commissions + unrecoverable withholding) in the base currency and as % of the portfolio. The honest question: does the committee add value, or should the core ETF grow?
5. **Tax rules on frequent or short-term sales:** some jurisdictions tax people who sell a lot or early differently (e.g. USA, long-term capital gains only after 1 year). Look up the rule for the country of residence in the official source and apply it to `transactions.csv`. Example for Switzerland — professional securities dealer status (Federal Tax Administration circular 36): compute in Python, from `transactions.csv`, the 5 safe-harbour criteria for the current year: (1) every security sold was held ≥ 6 months; (2) volume of purchases + sales ≤ 5× the portfolio value at the start of the year; (3) realised capital gains < 50% of the year's net income (ask the user for their income if unknown); (4) purchases not financed on credit, or interest covered by the securities' income; (5) derivatives only for hedging. For criterion (1), use the FIFO lots defined in the tax report below (BUY and OPENING_BALANCE with a ticker, except "split" BUYs, which inherit the original lot's date). Table ✅/⚠️/🔴. Failing a criterion does not automatically make the user a professional dealer, but it raises the risk of capital gains being taxed as income: flag 🔴 and say "confirm with the tax authority".
6. **Algorithm:** run `calc.py learn`. For each "TO CLOSE" record, look up the stock price and dividends per share (converted to the record's share basis if there was a split: 2:1 split → × 2) and the index level on the exact 12-month date, and the gross dividends per share over that period, and run `calc.py close`. Then run `learn` again and show the IC table. If there is a weights proposal, present it together with the lessons; save it to `weights.json` only after the user approves.
7. Propose **at most 3 new lessons** for `lessons.md` and, if needed, concrete changes to the agents' instructions (file and text). **Show the proposals and apply them only after the user approves.**
8. Report `reports/YYYY-MM-DD-review.md` and a REVIEW row in the journal, with V1, the cash, the index level (I1), the period return and the index return (in the Price on the day column), and the date: the next review starts from there (V0 = this V1, I0 = this I1).

## "tax report YYYY"
From `transactions.csv`: gross dividends by company and by country, withholding tax paid, following the rules and forms of the country of residence (look them up at the official tax authority and cite the source). Show what is recoverable under the double-taxation treaty between the company's country and the country of residence (e.g. for a Swiss resident: Switzerland 35% on the tax return; USA and other countries: credit through form DA-1; France and Spain: refund claim to that country) and what is lost. A table ready for the securities schedule of the country's tax return (Switzerland: *Wertschriftenverzeichnis*): security, ISIN if known, quantity at 31/12, tax value at 31/12, gross dividends, withholding. Always say "confirm with the tax authority". Foreign-currency dividends have gross and withholding in the original currency (in the note): convert them at the exchange rate the country's tax authority requires (Switzerland: the Federal Tax Administration's Kursliste for the payment date) and check against the broker's annual tax statement. **Realised capital gains** (if the country of residence taxes them; e.g. Portugal, Annex G; USA, short vs. long term): for each SELL of the year (except those with a "reverse split" note), compute in Python the gain or loss in the base currency by FIFO (lots = BUY and OPENING_BALANCE with a ticker, except BUYs with a "split" note; cost = the lot's `value_base` + `fee_base`; proceeds = the SELL's `value_base` − `fee_base`) and each lot's holding period. Splits and reverse splits change the quantity of existing lots; they neither create nor sell lots (rules in `references/data.md`). The same lot rules serve criterion (1) of the Swiss example above. Attach the table to the report. Save to `reports/YYYY-tax.md`.

## "annual letter YYYY" (in January, after the review and the tax report)
A letter to the shareholder (the user), in the style of Buffett's letters: honest, simple, no jargon.
1. **The year's performance** vs. the benchmark and since inception, and total costs. Chain the returns of the reviews (REVIEW rows in the journal): (1 + r1) × (1 + r2) × … − 1, and the same for the index. If the last review does not end on 31/12, first do a review up to that date.
2. **Look-through earnings:** our share of each company's earnings (owner earnings per share × shares held, in the base currency), summed, and the growth vs. the previous year. This, not the share price, shows whether the portfolio is becoming more valuable.
3. **Dividends received** and growth vs. the previous year.
4. **The year's decisions:** the best and the worst, including errors of omission (what we should have bought and did not), with the review's classification.
5. **What we learned** (link to `lessons.md`) and **what we will do differently**. No price forecasts.
Use `journal.md`, the last review and the tax report. New numbers: owner earnings per share from the `fundamental-analyst`; weights and values in the base currency from the `risk-manager`. Save to `reports/YYYY-annual-letter.md`.

## "show the dashboard"
Build or update a page with: portfolio value in the base currency, weights by company, sector, country and currency vs. the policy limits, core vs. satellites, expected annual dividends, performance vs. the index (if there is history), the watchlist with the distance to the buy price and upcoming calendar events. If the Artifact tool is available, use it; otherwise generate `reports/dashboard.html` (static HTML, no external dependencies) and give the path. Use the numbers from the last review (state the date) or ask the `risk-manager` for fresh ones.
